# Ledger Press

Ledger Press treats market shock as a printed artifact: precise, sober, and slightly theatrical. The work should feel like a financial newspaper page reduced to one decisive visual object, where every number has editorial weight and every line exists because it clarifies hierarchy.

The composition relies on a disciplined grid, wide margins, and restrained asymmetry. Typography carries the tension: a large serif headline anchors the event, while small clinical labels act like Bloomberg terminal annotations. The final piece must feel meticulously crafted, with the kind of spacing and alignment that looks labored over by a senior editorial designer.

Color is used as data, not decoration. Deep red marks regulatory penalty, green marks original earnings power, blue marks adjusted earnings, and muted paper tones hold the system together. The palette should feel institutional, not promotional.

The visual idea is compression: a complicated regulatory event distilled into four numbers and one sentence. It should avoid explanation-heavy text and instead let blocks, dividers, and numeric contrast communicate the model. The result should look painstakingly refined, as if it went through many editorial proofs before publication.
